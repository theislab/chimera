## Page 1: Overview: what the model takes, does and returns

Figure 1, key `main_overview`, label `fig:overview`.

A protein can stay almost the same between species while the cells use its gene differently, and a protein can change while the cells use it alike. Panel (a) shows these two cases with two toy genes: protein sequence on top, expression across cell states below. Panel (b) lists what the model is given: single-cell counts from several species for genes that have exactly one counterpart in each species, how similar the proteins are (the protein prior), and a description of each gene (protein language model, protein similarity, expression statistics). Panel (c) is the model. Cells of all species are placed on one map. Two decoders explain the counts together: one does not know the species, the other does. A gate per gene decides how much of the species-aware decoder is let in. The gate starts where the protein similarity puts it and moves only when the counts say so; that count evidence is called S_g. Panel (d) shows what comes out: the cell map, and for every gene the count evidence, the gate, and how far the gate ended from its protein-based start (Δα). Above the prior means the cells of the species share the gene's pattern; below means the pattern or the level differs. Genes with too few counts get a value from genes with similar proteins, shown in gray. Panel (e) lists what the paper does with these readouts.

## Page 2: The gate value α against the protein prior

Figure 2, key `main_alpha_vs_prior`, label `fig:alpha-intro`.

Every dot or bar is a gene. The model starts each gene at a value set by how similar its protein is between the species (the protein prior). During training the gene's value α can move away from that start when the counts say so. (a) α against the prior: most genes stay near the diagonal and many leave it. (b) The move itself, Δα = α minus the prior: about half of the genes move up and half move down. (c) The distribution of α beside that of the prior: the same overall shape, shifted by the data.

## Page 15: Training dynamics of α
